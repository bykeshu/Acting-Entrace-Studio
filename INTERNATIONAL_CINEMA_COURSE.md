# International cinema: an actor's starting course

Added 26 September 2026. **Supplementary preparation**, not an official FTII or NSD prescribed film list. Programme targets remain FTII MFA in Cinema, Screen Acting, and NSD New Delhi's three-year Diploma in Dramatics.

## Enjoyment comes first — preference updated 27 September 2026

Watch a film to enjoy it, feel it and live with its moments. Not every viewing is a learning opportunity, and it never has to become one. No viewing quota, compulsory dossier, rehearsal output or quiz follows a film. Choose by curiosity, mood, access and content comfort; pause or switch freely. Personal viewing is not marked as syllabus mastery or added to practice hours.

The separate [Pinterest bucket list](MOVIE_BUCKET_LIST.md) preserves your six saved lists, including catharsis and Van Gogh on screen (59 film/TV title labels in total). In the app, **Movie bucket list** is a personal shelf: a watched tick is enough. Keeping a feeling or moment is optional and ungraded. **World cinema** retains the cultural/acting references for when you actively want to explore them, after viewing.

## How to use this course — only when you choose to study

The twelve foundation films below are an optional starting map, not a timetable. There is no requirement to finish them or write notes. If you choose a study session, fit it around classes and use existing cinema-study time. Longer films may be split across sittings. No viewing history or learning weakness has been assumed.

1. Watch for the story and your own experience first. Original-language audio and reliable subtitles can help; do not assume subtitles preserve every vocal nuance.
2. Afterwards, if curious, read an institutional source or revisit an exchange for objective, obstacle, action, listening and tempo-rhythm.
3. Separate observable actor choices from your interpretation and from effects of framing, editing, music and offscreen sound.
4. Optional practice ideas: a short note **or** a safe 60–90-second **original** scene at stage and phone close-up scale. Neither is compulsory. No copyrighted dialogue is supplied. Do not recreate violence, drug use or sexual coercion.
5. If wanted, save a reflection in **World cinema**, or a feeling/moment in **Movie bucket list**. Mark **Watched** separately only if true. Excerpt study is valuable but does not count as viewing the feature or mastering the topic. Daily-coach assessment is separate and is not triggered by viewing.

Every film card supplies its edition year, director, primary language(s), approximate runtime, cultural context, actor lens, Bengal comparison, observation question, reference and a preliminary content heads-up. Content notes are not exhaustive certifications; consult detailed advice and substitute a title freely.

## Preparation connections — coach interpretations

- **FTII:** world-cinema awareness, director/actor knowledge, performance examples, framing, eyeline, continuity and actor-director choices. Existing modules: `ftii-cinema` in the research syllabus / `ftii-screen` in the app, plus `ftii-interview`.
- **NSD:** objective, conflict, text/character interpretation, voice/body choices, stage-screen scale and evidence-supported critical writing (`shared-theory`, `shared-analysis`, `nsd-written`). Film study does not replace prescribed-play reading or theatre experience.
- **Bengal:** compare specific choices with Ray, Ghatak or Mrinal Sen (`bengal-screen`). Neither a director nor a national cinema has only one performance style. Similarity does not prove historical influence.

Historical Soviet cinema is not simply relabelled contemporary Russia. The list is a starting sample, not a ranking or a claim that one film defines a country.

## Cult classics are not the same as important films

“Cult” describes reception and devoted audiences, not a quality score or synonym for every canonical film. [BFI / Sight and Sound explicitly calls Trainspotting a cult classic](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/reviews/t2-trainspotting-review-diluted-fix-96); [BFI also examines its wider pop-cultural impact](https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/trainspotting-phenomenon-20-years).

Two further sourced selections are [Stalker, whose cult success BFI discusses in relation to Tarkovsky's artistic legacy](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/features/tarkovsky-legacy), and [Mulholland Dr., described as a cult classic in Festival du nouveau cinéma's restoration programme](https://nouveaucinema.ca/storage/document/2022-08-22/1753-021043.pdf). These are reception judgements attributed to those sources, not universal labels.

The app's **Cult & experiments** shelf combines those three sourced cult selections with *8½*, *Persona* and *Jeanne Dielman* for studying formal experimentation. It does not claim that all of those works have the same reception history. Begin these after a few foundation films; confusion is a useful observation, not a reason to invent one definitive explanation.

## Legal access, editions and limitations

Metadata and source pages were checked on **26 September 2026**. Runtime follows the linked edition: e.g. Criterion lists *Tokyo Story* at 137 minutes and *Cléo* at 89; institutional/distributor catalogues may differ. *Timbuktu* is listed at 97 minutes by Film at Lincoln Center and Cohen, while the Cannes webpage lists 100. *City of God* is 130 minutes in the Siskel catalogue; Cannes lists 135. Do not invent scene timestamps across editions.

**India: lawful full-film availability not verified for this bibliography.** Distributor and archive pages are references, not proof of an Indian rental/subscription stream. No paid subscription is assumed, no copyrighted film is bundled, and no arbitrary uploaded copy is endorsed. Before a dated watch assignment, re-check the exact film's regional playback, language/subtitles and access type. A lawful excerpt is an acceptable substitute when access, time or content is unsuitable.

For a short comparison now, the production company's [Charulata page](https://www.rdborganization.com/rdb/charulata.html) links this [restored excerpt](https://vimeo.com/57357485). Study one response or gesture and separate what you see from what you infer. This is a Bengal comparison exercise, not a replacement international feature.

Gaps remain: documentary, animation, Indigenous cinemas, silent-film performance, more women directors, further African/Asian/Arab/Latin American regions and recent work after 2019. “Important” is a curatorial judgement, not an exhaustive national history. Future additions should have specific learning value and verified references rather than fame alone.
